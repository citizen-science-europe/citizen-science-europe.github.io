ECSAtech seminar

Can we build a citizen science monitoring platform in an hour?

Participants submitted freshwater observations from their phones and watched the results appear on a shared map. Explore the demonstrations and prompts.

What participants tried

Luigi Ceccaroni, Earthwatch innovation lead and ECSAtech co-chair, used FreshWater Watch as a starting point. The exercise asked whether a group with little or no software budget could collect observations on phones, store them centrally and view them on a shared map.

Submit an observation

The mobile form captured a site name, coordinates and an observation date. Participants could add a time, photograph, notes, participant count and freshwater observations.

Watch the map update

The map refreshed every five seconds. Selecting a marker opened its record. The presenter also encountered a pending upload while using a weak connection.

Review and export the data

Visitors could inspect records and export a CSV. Owner sign-in enabled editing; the presenter demonstrated removing a photograph from a record.

A quick prototype and a deployment the team could control

An earlier ChatGPT run produced a prototype at a temporary address in about 50 minutes. The Cloudflare and Supabase version that participants tested took about three hours to prepare. It used two separate prompts: one to prepare the accounts and publishing setup, followed by one to build and deploy the application.

The session also showed a Gemini build deployed with Google Sites and Firebase, and a Z.ai prototype with a tabbed form and sample records. A fresh ChatGPT run during the session did not produce the planned live result.

What would it take to use this in a project?

The group discussed where observations and photographs are stored, who can see precise coordinates, and who will maintain the site when tools or service plans change. Participants also raised GDPR and FAIR data: making data findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable. Free software can help keep costs down, while fieldwork can still require test kits and consumables.
