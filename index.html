ECSAtech seminar · 16 November 2026

Can we build a citizen science monitoring platform in an hour?

The challenge: use AI to go from a blank page to a working prototype with a mobile-friendly survey and an online map that updates as observations are submitted, using free tools.

Monday 16 November 2026, 15:00 UK time / 16:00 CET · Online seminar in English

Go to the session materials page

From a freshwater survey to a live map

Luigi Ceccaroni, innovation lead at Earthwatch, set out to show how several AI tools could help design and build a citizen science monitoring platform. The RiverWatch web app and FreshWater Watch served as starting examples for designing a tool people could use to record observations in the field.

Design the survey

Decide what volunteers need to capture, from site details and visible conditions to water-quality observations.

Build for field use

Use AI to develop a mobile-friendly prototype that lets people submit observations from their phones.

Show observations on a map

Connect the survey to an online map so that new submissions can be displayed as they come in.

Freshwater was the example. The same design exercise could start with a question about biodiversity, astronomy or another citizen science topic. The seminar was open to the whole ECSA community, with no prior experience in environmental monitoring or app building required.

The seminar was set up for automatic transcription, with no recording planned. The session materials page is reserved for resources that can be shared separately.

About ECSAtech

The ECSA Projects, Data, Tools and Technology working group explores how technology can make citizen science more accessible, collaborative and effective. Existing projects provide examples for testing ideas that other teams can adapt.
